WODForge Privacy Policy

Effective October 15, 2026

WODForge is a personal workout app that is currently in beta testing. This policy explains what information the app handles, where it is stored, and the choices you have. The short version: your profile, health notes and workout history stay on your iPhone. We do not run accounts, analytics, advertising or tracking.

Who we are

WODForge is developed by an independent developer (the "developer", "we", "us"). You can reach us at theensoway@gmail.com.

Information stored on your device

To build workouts that fit you, the app stores the following information locally on your device: your first name (optional), age, height, weight, units, activity level and experience level; health and injury flags and any notes you add; your training places and equipment; generated workouts; workout logs (scores, loads, effort, how you felt, pain flags and notes); saved timer presets; and app settings.

This information is saved in the app's private storage on your device. It is not sent to us, and we cannot see it.

Health information

Health and injury flags, pain reports and notes are used only on your device to remove or swap movements that may not suit you. They are never uploaded, sold, shared or used for advertising. WODForge does not read from or write to Apple Health.

WODForge is not a medical device and does not provide medical advice. Talk to a qualified professional before starting a new exercise program, and stop if something hurts.

Services that receive limited technical information

Exercise demo videos (YouTube): demonstration videos are embedded from YouTube using its privacy-enhanced youtube-nocookie.com player. When you open an exercise, your device connects to YouTube, which receives standard technical information such as your IP address and device and browser details, and may collect information about video playback. YouTube is operated by Google and handles that information under the Google Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy).

App updates (Expo): the app checks Expo's update service (expo.dev) for bug fixes and improvements. These requests include standard technical information such as your IP address, the app version and platform, and a random installation identifier that Expo uses to count active installs. They do not include your profile, health information or workout history. See the Expo Privacy Policy (https://expo.dev/privacy).

Beta testing (Apple TestFlight): if you join the beta through TestFlight, Apple shares certain information with us, such as your name and email address (if you were invited by email), your device model and iOS version, crash reports and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send through TestFlight. Apple handles this information under its own privacy policy (https://www.apple.com/legal/privacy/).

On-device speech: voice prompts in the timer use your iPhone's built-in text-to-speech.

Feedback you send us

If you email us, for example by tapping Send beta feedback, we receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to reply and to improve WODForge, and we delete beta feedback emails when you ask or within 12 months after the beta ends.

What we do not do

We do not create accounts, sell or rent personal information, show ads, use third-party analytics or advertising SDKs, or track you across other companies' apps and websites.

Keeping and deleting your information

On-device data stays until you delete it. You can erase everything at any time in Profile, then Beta, then Reset all data, or by deleting the app.

To leave the beta and stop TestFlight sharing, open the TestFlight app, select WODForge and choose Stop Testing. To have your feedback emails deleted, email theensoway@gmail.com.

Children

WODForge is not intended for children under 13, and the app asks for an age of 13 or older during setup. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to this policy

If we add features that send your information off your device, such as accounts or cloud backup, we will update this policy before the change takes effect and ask for your permission in the app first. The effective date at the top always shows the latest version.
